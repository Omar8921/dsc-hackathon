## Patterns
1. **Visible transformation in seconds.** Brainwaves → music, blinks → text, video → alert, any input → RPG. The judge sees input go in and something surprising come out, live.
2. **The AI uses a camera, a microphone or a sensor, or it generates something interactive.** Almost none of the winners is a chat window.
3. **A specific human beneficiary**: a blind person, a foster youth, a person who can't speak. It's specific, and you can feel it.
4. **Judges can touch it.** The best demos let the judge try it themselves.
5. **One loop, fully working.** lablab's guide: if a judge can't grasp the value from the opening screen, "you've already lost points"; get a working prototype within the first ~24 h. ([lablab](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon))
6. **Startup framing at AI hackathons:** judges score technology, presentation, business value and originality. "Think startup pitch, not benchmark run." ([lablab AMD](https://lablab.ai/ai-hackathons/amd-developer-hackathon-act-ii))

## What this means for us (7-min demo, judges: domain + AI + business)
- Our demo should have a **"whoa" moment in the first 60 seconds**, ideally with the **judges participating** (scan a QR code, try it on their own phone).
- Output should be **interactive**, not text.
- The beneficiary must still answer the society-sector question: **"who is left out today?"**
